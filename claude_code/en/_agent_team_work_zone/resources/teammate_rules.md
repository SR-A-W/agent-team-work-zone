## Teammate Rules (essentials)

> This block is maintained by the framework and is replaced as a whole on upgrade; local edits will be overwritten (the old block is backed up as `README.md.teammate_rules.bak.<timestamp>`).

1. **Only touch your own workstation** — your workstation is `<team>/teammates/<your-name>/`; only you maintain its 5 files. **Do not** modify another teammate's, the lead's, or anyone else's workstation files; to get someone else to do something, send them a SendMessage. Peer-to-peer communication and collaboration (asking questions, sharing, challenging, helping each other) is encouraged and is a core value of the team; but formal task assignment and prioritization is the lead's coordination responsibility — don't hand tasks to peers as if they were orders.
2. **Cross-agent communication follows Claude Code's official agent-team mechanism (mailbox / SendMessage)** — per the official mechanism, agents communicate only via mailbox delivery (the SendMessage tool); your plain output **does not cross the agent boundary** to reach the lead — it exists only in your own session, visible only if a human user looks at your pane/transcript. Reporting progress, asking questions, delivering work **must** go through SendMessage, or it didn't happen. (When you go idle, the system notifies the lead automatically, but that's a content-free heartbeat — it **cannot substitute** for your report.) **A successful send means delivered; silence ≠ loss — do not resend just because you haven't gotten a reply yet.** SendMessage returning `success:true` (`Message sent to X's inbox`) means it's already in the recipient's mailbox; no immediate reply usually means they're busy / haven't gotten to it, not that the message was lost. **Resend in only two cases**: (i) SendMessage itself **errored** (e.g. `No agent named X is currently addressable`); (ii) the recipient **explicitly says it didn't arrive**. Otherwise do not re-spam the same message. When messaging the lead, set `to:` to the lead's **registered name** (the one in your spawn / reactivation prompt, typically `team-lead`), copied **verbatim — do not invent an address**.
3. **Checkpointing is a standing obligation** — after task completion / before going idle / when prompted → call `/checkpoint` to update `working-context.md`. It's your handoff note to your next-spawned self; Claude Code does **not** preserve teammate sessions across restarts, so a poorly written one means the next you can't recover. `commitments.md` holds your promises to others — the next you must pick those up. Don't rely solely on the 15-minute automatic reminder as a backstop.
4. **After compression, recover from your workstation files, not memory** — after context compression, read your own workstation to recover state: `README.md` (role identity), `working-context.md` (work state), `commitments.md` (outstanding promises), `TODO.md` (pending items). Don't guess from residual memory.
5. **Task tracking lives on disk in your own workstation** — `TODO.md` / `ACTIVE_JOBS.md` / `COMPLETED_JOBS.md` belong in your workstation directory; **do not** use `~/.claude/tasks/` (session-scoped — gone the moment the conversation ends).
6. **When in doubt, ask the lead — not the user directly** — if requirements, purpose, or direction are unclear, use SendMessage to ask the team lead; the lead relays genuinely major questions to the user. **A wrong assumption costs far more than one extra question**; **never pretend you already have agreement**. Also **don't stop and wait idle for the user to reply** — the user usually only watches the lead's session (or is in remote-control/non-tmux mode) and can't see your question at all; you waiting on the user while the lead waits on your deliverable is a whole-team deadlock.
7. **What goes into roundtable** — reports must be self-contained; filename format `<your-name>_<type>_<YYYYMMDD>_<HHMM>_<description>.md`; **archiving rights belong solely to the issuer** — if you didn't post it, don't archive it; if you did post it and every recipient has marked it RESOLVED, you archive it.
8. **To watch a job and stay reachable, schedule a wake-up with `/loop`, then end your turn** — messages from teammates reach you only when your turn ends. Do not wait inside the turn (a shell `until … sleep` loop, or a chain of checks): neither a long blocking call nor a chain of short checks lets messages through; only ending the turn does. Ending the turn without a scheduled wake-up leaves the job unwatched; if you cannot use `/loop`, end the turn with a short status to the lead, who pings you to continue.
9. **Record where decisions came from** — when you note a ruling or agreement, state who proposed and who approved, quoting "who decided" verbatim. Anything you and a peer agree on a contract, interface, or field is a proposal until the lead rules; record it as "proposal (pending lead ruling)".
10. **The working tree and index are shared by the whole team** — check `git diff --cached --stat` before committing and commit only by explicit path (`git commit -m "…" -- <path>`); never `git add -A` / `add .` / `commit -a`, `git stash`, `pull --rebase`, or a non-fast-forward merge; never delete an existing `.git/index.lock` (if it persists with no git process running, tell the lead). For commits, merges and pulls in the shared checkout, prefer `cd "<project root>" && bash _agent_team_work_zone/resources/scripts/atwz_git_lock.sh run -- git …`, where `<project root>` is the absolute directory that contains `_agent_team_work_zone/` (your spawn prompt gives it); this works from any working directory, even when the project is a subdirectory of a larger repository.

This is a condensed excerpt for teammates; see the framework README's "Work Rules" for the full 13.
